# Taste Model

## Sensibility
- Ideas land hardest when a single character must live them out under pressure. Evidence: *Crime and Punishment*, both *Notes from Underground* volumes, *The Picture of Dorian Gray* and *Père Goriot*, all rated 5.
- Argument lands best in a combative, personal voice. Nietzsche's *The Will to Power* (5) and Lewis's *Mere Christianity* (5) outscore Goethe's *Maxims and Reflections* (2) and Marcus Aurelius (3.5).
- Argument dramatised inside a fictional frame is a sweet spot. Evidence: *The Screwtape Letters* (5), *Brave New World* (4.5) and *The Great Divorce* (4).
- History lands through one life rather than a period or institution. *Napoleon the Great* and *Churchill* (5) outscore *China's Cosmopolitan Empire* (2) and *Lords of the Horizons* (3).
- A biographer with a strong verdict beats a measured one. Roberts and Jung Chang (5) outrank Massie's *Catherine*, Goldsworthy's *Caesar* and Hurd's *Peel* (3–3.5).
- First-person testimony from inside catastrophe is a top register. Evidence: *The Choice* and *Wild Swans* (5).
- Practical advice needs authority earned in high-stakes practice. Voss (5), McChrystal and McMaster (4) outrank the copywriting and StoryBrand manuals (3).
- Length is no obstacle, since 900–1,100-page books score 5.
- Earnest moral seriousness beats comic irony.
- Epic narrative poetry lands and lyric does not yet. The evidence is Homer (4.5) against Wilde's poems (3), which is thin.

## Territory
- Dostoevsky is the anchor, with five volumes and none rated below 4.
- Nineteenth-century realism is a core territory: Balzac, Flaubert, Tolstoy, the Brontës and Hawthorne.
- Lewis-centred Christian apologetics is a core territory, with four Lewis books, a Lewis biography and Spurgeon.
- Nietzsche is a core author, with four books.
- Great-man political and military biography recurs, especially Andrew Roberts (three books).
- Twentieth-century totalitarianism recurs: Chang twice, Mussolini, and *Brave New World*.
- Conservative polemic recurs: three Douglas Murray books and two by Peterson.
- Self-improvement, marriage, investing (Graham twice) and tech management are read steadily at 3–4 without deep commitment.
- Irish history recurs at arm's length (Richard English, Bhreathnach, *Bloody Sunday*).

## Rejections
- *Heart of Darkness* (1). Hypothesis: oblique, impressionistic narration withholds the moral clarity they want.
- *Portnoy's Complaint* and *The Satsuma Complex* (2). Hypothesis: comic confession without metaphysical stakes reads as trivial.
- *Maxims and Reflections* (2). Hypothesis: aphorism without a combative persona feels inert.
- *China's Cosmopolitan Empire* (2). Hypothesis: thematic history with no central figure fails them.
- *The Norse Mythology Bible* (2). Hypothesis: the problem is low-quality compilation, not myth, given Homer at 4.5.
- Contemporary literary fiction is nearly absent. Hypothesis: this is avoidance, not dislike, since *Atonement* scored 4.

## Tensions
- Nietzsche and Lewis both score 5. They may want the strongest case on each side of the God question rather than a settled answer.
- Roberts admires Napoleon while Chang indicts Mao. The common thread is power concentrated in one person and judged hard, either way.
- Dostoevsky sits beside Goggins and *Atomic Habits*. Both may serve one question, how to live, asked at two altitudes.
- Christian apologetics scores 5 while the Gita and Buddhist scriptures score 3–3.5. Transcendence seems to land in a Western argumentative idiom, not a contemplative one.

## Directions
**Biosecurity.** This is absent from the history. Enter through insider testimony and decision-failure narrative, modelled on *Dereliction of Duty*. This relies on the testimony and practitioner-authority claims. Ken Alibek's defector memoir fits. Avoid policy reports and threat surveys.

**AI.** This is adjacent only through *The Innovator's Dilemma* and *Team Topologies*. Enter through builders written as characters, and through the question of what humans are for. This relies on the single-life and dramatised-argument claims. Avoid hype and futurist listicles.

**Synthetic biology.** *The Moral Animal* (4) shows science lands when tied to human nature. Enter through a discoverer's biography, such as Walter Isaacson on Doudna. This relies on the single-life claim. Avoid textbook-style surveys.

**Poetry.** Extend the epic-narrative success. Dante joins epic form to Christian moral argument. Browning's dramatic monologues carry argument in voice. Avoid short lyric collections as a first step.

**Classic fiction.** This is well represented, so push sideways within moral-psychological realism. Stendhal's ambitious young man is kin to Balzac's Rastignac. George Eliot adds moral seriousness at novel scale. Avoid picaresque and impressionistic modernism for now.

**History.** Push from admired lives to lives prosecuted. Robert Caro gives one life carrying institutional power at great length. Simon Sebag Montefiore offers Chang-style verdicts on tyranny. Avoid dynastic or thematic surveys.
